# Gameplay Design

Read [the shared Game Designer contract](../../../agents/game-designer.md) before acting, including on direct invocation. Read the current Design Brief/project record first. Require its confirmed Design Brief checkpoint, document language, and explicit approval reference; otherwise return to `design-intake`.

Use [the gameplay proposal template](templates/gameplay-proposal.md). Keep rule definitions out of this proposal: name the authoritative system-specification rule IDs once they exist.

## Design

1. When the confirmed brief leaves the concept open, compare exactly three materially different experience candidates before describing a singular loop or mechanic. Each names its hook, player action, primary creative priority, intentional sacrifice, and largest uncertainty. Recommend one with rationale, but leave selection and approval to the user. Do not substitute implementation ease, tile changes, or theme wrappers for distinct experiences. Read [design approaches](../../../agents/references/design-approaches.md) only when selecting an experience-specific lens or a smallest experiment.
2. After that comparison, describe only the recommended candidate's provisional player-facing loop, choices, stakes, and observable outcomes. For each optional destination, branch, or encounter, name the player-visible promise before entry, the unresolved question it creates, and what distinguishes it from its siblings. A generic label, darkness, or mere presence is not a sufficient promise. When tension or pressure is intended, distinguish the experiential role of atmosphere, spatial commitment, punishment, and numeric management; defer authoritative mechanics to `system-design`. Classify supplied facts, Design Assumptions, and unvalidated experience claims. Keep it proposed until the user explicitly selects it and confirms the existing combined core-gameplay/prototype-scope checkpoint.
3. When the brief already selects a concept, record its explicit selection reference with its primary creative priority and intentional sacrifice, then develop that direction directly. For a bounded in-scope fix, reference those existing choices rather than reopening concept competition or inventing a new selection.
4. Before recommending a build, work through a concrete player decision: what the player notices, tries, changes, and can do next. Compare the consequences of two plausible actions under the proposed mechanic; for a deliberately single-solution experience, compare the first attempt with an informed attempt instead. Tie that difference to the selected creative priority. When authorship or divergent player agency is claimed but both actions merely unlock the same prescribed result, describe access rather than authorship and resolve the mismatch before proposing implementation. Offer alternatives only when they could materially improve the agreed experience, with their benefit, production or learning risk, and low-cost validation experiment.
5. Specify the minimum playable scope, validation question, local technology assumption, excluded work, and the project's supplied iteration limit. The validation question must name two separate observable signals: initial attraction (does the player start or investigate unprompted) and sustained interest (does the player continue or return after the first payoff-free moment). For a build, reshape, or route-changing mechanic, also name at least two materially different player-authored states, routes, or outcomes and ask what each enables; merely opening the intended route does not establish creative authorship. If validation finds access without authorship, stop that direction rather than adding equivalent gates or interactables. If it is missing, propose a conservative limit for confirmation; never replace an explicit user budget. Treat technical architecture and effort estimates as tentative until developer confirmation. A single solution or no replay loop is valid when it serves the intended experience.
6. Identify the weakest link in the worked decision and one observable result that would reject the design hypothesis. Choose the cheapest test of that link: a paper walkthrough of design causality, one playable interaction, or a comparison of variants when the approved budget permits. A designer walkthrough is reasoning, not Player Validation. For learning or mastery claims, include a later situation that tests transfer of the learned action; otherwise state the applicable experiential progression. Submit core gameplay and prototype scope together for checkpoint confirmation. Keep the checkpoint `pending` until an explicit user approval reference is recorded; do not self-approve or start work that exceeds the confirmed budget.

## Result

Produce a concise proposal developers can challenge and a handoff to `system-design` after the core gameplay and prototype scope checkpoint is confirmed. Keep narrative, art, audio, and level detail only where it affects the loop or readable player feedback.
