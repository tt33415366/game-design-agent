# Designer Behavior Acceptance: Exploration Slice

## Purpose

Validate whether the applied F9-F16 guidance changes a designer response before any new prototype is authorized. This is an instruction-only exercise, not a game proposal, selected concept, prototype scope, or Player Validation.

## Controlled Brief

- Player desire: free underwater exploration with mostly ease and brief, local pressure.
- World condition: three optional side spaces branch from a central reef route.
- Mechanic candidate: the player can reposition two current stones.
- Constraint: no meters, countdowns, damage, failure state, or prototype implementation.

## Acceptance Conditions

| Lesson | Required response behavior | Baseline failure evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F9/F14 authorship | Reject a solution that only opens a prescribed route; name two materially different player-authored states/routes/outcomes and what each enables. | Three Stones U4: access was restored but did not feel creative. |
| F10 opportunity promise | Give each optional space a visible pre-entry promise, unresolved question, and distinction from its siblings. | Echo Caves U1: entrances existed but did not pull exploration. |
| F11 promise-to-payoff chain | Plan observation of `notice -> enter or avoid -> payoff or retreat -> next choice`, including player reason. | Echo Caves U1: payoff, rather than entrance cue, drove the next entry. |
| F12 pressure semantics | Separate atmospheric cue and spatial commitment from punishment/fail state and numeric resource; name the deliberate exclusions. | Sunlight Shelf and Echo Caves: pressure was intended as atmosphere, not management. |
| F13 interaction path | Require a live path from approach through exit after the changed state, rather than helper-only confirmation. | Three Stones U1/U2: unreachable object then post-push trap. |
| F15 evidence strength | Mark a statement such as “works better” as relative improvement only and state the unproven signal. | Three Stones U3 and Echo Caves U2. |
| F16 independence | Declare this response self-validated and queue an independent recheck rather than claiming independent verification. | Previous worker delegation failures. |

## Response Under Test

### Opportunity and Authorship

1. **Echo arch:** a cold current visibly bends kelp into an opening, then briefly carries a distant bell-like note. Question: does the current lead to a living chamber or a buried mechanism? It differs by promising motion and sound, not only a darker tunnel.
2. **Sunlit chimney:** a narrow shaft flickers with surface caustics and releases rising bubbles. Question: is the light an exit, an airless ruin, or a route to a new viewpoint? It differs by promising vertical travel and a changed perspective.
3. **Silt garden:** suspended particles settle in a circular pattern around immovable coral. Question: what is creating the pattern below the silt? It differs by promising a concealed process rather than traversal alone.

Repositioning one current stone must create at least two valid authored outcomes: divert flow to reveal and safely reach the Echo arch while leaving the chimney calm, or redirect flow upward to make the chimney traversable while obscuring the Echo arch. Each outcome changes the player’s next opportunity and not only their access to a prescribed gate. A design that merely moves a block from the central route fails the authorship condition and should stop.

### Pressure Semantics

- Atmospheric cue: sound dampens, light narrows, and particles stream faster near redirected flow.
- Spatial commitment: choosing the chimney commits the player to a vertical route until the next ledge; choosing the Echo arch commits them to a lateral current.
- Punishment/fail state: none.
- Numeric resource: none.
- Deliberate exclusion: no oxygen meter, countdown, damage, or forced retreat. The intended pressure is anticipation of movement and position, not resource management.

### Required Future Observation

For each branch, observe `notice cue -> enter or avoid -> encounter payoff or retreat -> choose next opportunity`, then record the stated or observable reason for the next choice. For each current-stone arrangement, exercise `approach -> initiate -> complete -> leave changed state` with live input and verify safe exit.

## Result

- Response coverage: `pass` for F9-F16 at the instruction level.
- Evidence strength: `self-validated instruction response only`; no Player Validation, implementation verification, or desirability result.
- Independent verification: `deferred`; require a separate reviewer or future behavior run to assess compliance without prompt-side assistance.
- Next decision: run one independent behavior review of this exact exercise before selecting or prototyping a new game concept.
